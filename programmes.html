System three · the programme portfolio

Fifteen programmes, and a rule for funding them.

Every programme names the seat it is aimed at, the mindset it speaks to, and the outcome it is judged on. Anything that cannot name all three does not run.

Fifteen sounds like a lot until you look at the map. It is five per stage, and most operate against a single buyer mindset, which means at any moment only a handful are competing for attention. The restraint is not in the count. It is that nothing was added because it is customary, and four things a regional budget usually funds are declined outright.

Six of the fifteen carry a note on where I have run something equivalent before. The other nine do not, because I would rather show you an honest portfolio than a decorated one.

The funding rule

What I would decline to fund in year one.

With one permanent hire and a contract bench, what gets declined says more about judgement than what gets approved. These are the four things a regional marketing budget is most often spent on, and I would not spend it on any of them.

Large-format trade show booths
High cost, badge-scan measurement, and they reach the wrong seat entirely. Curated meetings and hospitality at the same events deliver considerably more against open pipeline for a fraction of the outlay.
Regional brand advertising
Slow to read, hard to attribute, and it solves an awareness problem that does not exist. Anthropic is already known in this region. What it is not yet is believed on enterprise deployment, and that is a proof problem rather than a reach problem.
A second flagship event
One owned regional moment done properly is worth considerably more than two done adequately. The second one gets funded when the first has a measured result behind it, and not before.
Broad content localisation
Translating the full library is a large bill with weak returns. Localise only what the funded verticals are actually using in live deals, and let demand decide what follows.
