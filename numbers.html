System four · the measurement ladder

Two outcomes, and everything explains one of them.

Marketing is accountable for whether the region's regard for Anthropic moves, and whether that regard converts. Neither is permitted to stand in for the other. Budget and headcount follow from those two numbers rather than sitting beside them.

Most regional marketing reporting describes activity. It says how many events ran and how many people attended, and when a number misses it offers more activity as the remedy. That is not measurement, it is accounting.

The system below is built so that performance is explainable. Two outcomes at the top, ten signals beneath them, and every programme metric laddering to one of those signals. When something misses, the ladder tells you where to look.

Pipeline is modelled at ten times programme spend. The largest portfolio I have run had a $20 million budget, of which roughly half was aimed at pipeline and the rest at brand, certification and enablement. It carried an $80 million pipeline target and delivered 167% of it, which is a little under seven times total spend and around thirteen times the revenue-directed half.

Ten times total spend is a step up on that, and the difference has to be argued rather than assumed. Enterprise AI deal sizes are materially larger, consumption expands after close in a way a licence does not, and a partner-led portfolio in this region reaches accounts a direct one does not. If those three things do not hold, the number comes down.

The brand target is deliberately not scaled with spend. Consideration share moves slowly regardless of budget, and committing to a number before establishing a baseline is exactly the failure we ask customers to avoid.

Budget

Weighted toward what has to be proved first.

Expressed as percentages, because the split is the judgement and the total is an input. Modelled here on $20 million for year one, which is what competing at enterprise level across five national markets actually costs. There is a ceiling on how much demand capture can convert until local proof exists to convert it with, which is why credibility is the largest allocation in year one and would not be in year three.

Percentages are the recommendation. The final column rescales against whatever the real budget turns out to be.

People

One permanent hire, and three contract specialists.

A $20 million budget would lead most people to ask for a team of six. I would rather run it with contract specialists in the three execution disciplines and keep the permanent headcount at one until the volume proves itself.

The reason is not cost. It is that contract capacity flexes with the calendar, carries no ramp, and does not require a headcount approval cycle to start or to stop. A founding function does not yet know which markets will absorb the most activity, and hiring permanent people against a guess is how regional teams end up carrying the wrong shape for two years. Permanent roles get created once the work has demonstrated where it actually sits.

Nearly a third of the budget stays with the permanent role rather than being contracted out, and that is the part of the split that matters. Contractors execute against a defined brief. They cannot commission a customer story, hold a partner relationship at principal level, or decide what the region says about itself.

What cannot be contracted out

Narrative and messaging. Partner relationships at principal level. Sales alignment and the pipeline council. Commissioning customer proof. Measurement design. These are judgement calls and they cannot be contracted out without losing the thing that makes the system work.

Runs through partners

Workshops, builder events, certification delivery, and a share of vertical campaigns. Partners hold the local reach and the local trust. The job is to fund and equip them rather than to replicate them with headcount.

Runs through contract and agency

Execution across events, partner operations and field delivery, plus production, design, translation and analyst relations support. Capacity that scales with the calendar instead of sitting fixed on the payroll.

15
The size of the team I ran at Meta, including contractors, at a similar level of spend. I have managed spend at this level before, and the lesson from doing it is that leverage comes from partners and contract capacity rather than from permanent headcount added early.
