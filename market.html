System one · the two-layer model

APAC enterprises buy on trust, credibility and partnership.

Enterprise AI is a long-term bet on a company rather than a comparison of features, and in this region the evidence that settles it has to be local.

A San Francisco reference does not settle a Japanese consensus process. Korean enterprises take their lead from precedent set by their own conglomerates. Australian public sector buyers want regulatory standing established before they will look at a product. Indian buyers want the economics proven at their own volume rather than someone else's. Southeast Asian buyers move faster and more commercially, and the entry point there is usually a digital-native business rather than an incumbent. And across all five, a far larger share of the buying runs through system integrators than it does in the United States.

Asia Pacific is five buying cultures rather than one region, and the proof has to be built inside each of them. That is a brand to be built rather than a funnel to be filled.

The system I use to read this has two layers. The first is what the buyer is thinking, which tells you what to say. The second is what we have to prove to them, which tells you what to build. Most organisations run one layer and wonder why their pipeline reports do not explain their results.

The value is in the disagreement between them. An account can be down to a two-horse shortlist and still be blocked on credibility, because the finance function does not believe the number. It looks late-stage on every dashboard and it will not close. No amount of closing activity fixes it, and the cause is invisible unless you are reading both layers at once.

Layer one

What the buyer is thinking.

Nobody in this region is unaware of AI, so the top of the conventional awareness cycle is empty. The unawareness sits one level down, and it concerns what enterprise adoption actually requires.

Layer two

What we have to prove.

Three stages. Sequential for any single account, concurrent for the function, because the portfolio is always spread across all three.

Credibility

What it is
Being believed on enterprise deployment by organisations that have not yet deployed at scale.
What it is not
Awareness. They already know who Anthropic is. Buying reach solves a problem that does not exist.
What it must prove
That comparable organisations, in their market and their industry, moved from scattered individual use to deployed capability, and what measurably changed when they did.
Working when
Inbound conversations open with how rather than who.

Consideration

What it is
Making the business case buildable in a form the customer's own finance function will accept.
What it is not
A feature comparison. The blocker is rarely capability. It is that returns are hard to attribute and nobody can produce a defensible number.
What it must prove
That a case can be constructed and survive budget review, and that security, sovereignty and residency are settled rather than outstanding.
Working when
Committee coverage widens beyond the original sponsor and assurance questions arrive already answered.

Capture

What it is
Closing the deal and landing it far enough that the deployment produces a measured outcome.
What it is not
A handover at signature. Signature produces no proof, so marketing stays involved until there is a number worth reusing.
What it must prove
That the deployment works in production and generates a result the customer will stand behind publicly.
Working when
Cycle times fall, referenced deals close faster, and new proof enters the library every quarter.
